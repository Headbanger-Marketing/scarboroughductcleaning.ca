Combining what different people in the household have noticed into one request

Each person in your Scarborough home may remember something different: a dusty register, a longer dryer cycle, a hum from the fan. Each memory can show what one person saw or heard, though it may cover only part of the picture; a single request that merges them can hide who saw what and while what was running. Write each report in the words of the person who made it, mark anything passed on second-hand, and name one contact who can answer a provider's questions.

  1. Return grilles

    Openings that pull room air back into the system.

  2. Return ducts

    The runs leading from each grille to the furnace.

  3. Furnace blower and coil

    The blower, evaporator coil and cabinet.

  4. Supply trunk

    The largest duct carrying air away from the furnace.

  5. Branch runs

    The individual runs that split off toward each room.

  6. Supply registers

    Where air comes back into each room.

Questions About Your Scarborough Request

Who would actually clean the ducts if I contact Scarborough Duct Cleaning?

With no office, crew or vehicles, this site cannot send anyone to your home or quote a price: it is an enquiry website for Scarborough, not a contractor. Our enquiry team reviews each request. If one goes on to a provider, that provider must identify themselves and confirm they cover your address before anything is arranged.

Who reads my Scarborough request once it is sent, and what can they do with it?

You still decide whether to go ahead: any provider who responds has to agree the price, the timing and a written scope with you first, and you can decline at that point before any work has started. What you send is a description for our enquiry team to review. It does not arrange a visit, set a price or confirm that a provider covers your address.

Who should write the request when several people in my household noticed different things?

Choose one adult to write the request and act as the named contact. That person should copy each report in the words of whoever made it, marking anything heard second-hand, instead of summarizing them into a single account. The provider can then ask the contact follow-up questions and know which household member to ask about each detail.

What if two people in my Scarborough home disagree about what they noticed?

List both accounts as they were given and do not choose between them or guess a cause. A difference may simply reflect where each person was standing or what was running at that moment. Note the room, the time and the equipment in use for each, and let the provider decide what needs checking when the system is assessed.

Should I include something a family member told me but I did not see myself?

Yes, but label it as second-hand and say who told you. A provider can weigh a first-hand observation differently from a relayed one, and a clear label stops a rumour from turning into a fact. If possible, ask the original person to add a line in their own words, with the room and the time they noticed it.

Can one Scarborough request cover the dryer vent and the ducts together?

Yes, one request can describe several systems, but the dryer vent is a separate run from the furnace ducts and may be scoped separately. List each observation under the system it seems to concern, and the responding provider can then say what each part would involve. Nothing is agreed until a written scope covers every item you want included.

Which details help most when several people noticed different things?

Where, when and while what was running matter more than a theory about the cause. Add the room, the time of day, whether the fan, dryer or ventilator was on, and a photograph of any register, hood or label someone mentioned. If anyone smells gas, do not wait for a reply: leave the home and contact the gas supplier or emergency responders.

Who can authorize the work if I rent my Scarborough home?

Anyone can send a request, but only the person with authority over the property can approve work on it. If you rent, ask the landlord or property manager who may authorize cleaning and who provides access, and give the provider that contact. Include the household's observations either way so the review has something concrete to start from.
